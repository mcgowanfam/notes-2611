Location story · read aloud
Look up at this red-brick Gothic palace. In Chamber of Secrets, when Harry and Ron can't get through the barrier to Platform 9¾, they run back to the flying Ford Anglia and take off right over this station, past the clock tower. The films used St Pancras as the outside of "King's Cross" because it looked far more magical than the real thing next door. Three Muggle facts: the vast train shed behind it, designed by William Barlow, was finished in 1868 with a single 74-metre span, the largest structure of its kind at the time. The hotel front, George Gilbert Scott's Midland Grand, opened in 1873. And in the 1960s British Rail wanted to knock it all down; the poet John Betjeman led the campaign that got it Grade I listed in 1967, just days before demolition was due.

Location story · read aloud
Half-Blood Prince opens with Death Eaters streaking across London like black smoke and twisting this very bridge until it snaps and drops into the Thames. The crew closed the bridge and filmed here in March 2008, but the destruction itself was created with computer effects, so the real bridge survived just fine. Muggle facts: when it opened on 10 June 2000, it was the first new Thames crossing in central London since Tower Bridge in 1894. Within days it had to close, because the crowds' footsteps made it sway, and Londoners nicknamed it the Wobbly Bridge. It reopened in February 2002 after engineers fitted 91 dampers. It was designed by Foster + Partners with the sculptor Sir Anthony Caro and the engineers Arup.

Tap in and out with contactless, a phone wallet or Oyster. One card or device per person. Zones 1–2 daily cap is £8.90 per person (TfL). Peak fares weekdays 6:30–9:30 a.m. and 4–7 p.m. Uber can't pick up at Heathrow arrivals or on pedestrian streets.
